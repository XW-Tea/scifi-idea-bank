HOW TO NAVIGATE

  • SCROLL — fly along the way you are looking. Turn to face an island and push the wheel and you travel toward it; face straight ahead and it is the old glide down the river of time.
  • DRAG — hold and drag to look around: a full 360° turn, and up to 80° up or down. Turn right round to watch the years you have passed recede, or tip up and down to find the islands above and below you.
  • ARROWS, PAGE UP / PAGE DOWN, HOME / END — pure time travel: these only ever move along the time axis, whichever way you are facing. Scroll to explore, arrow keys to scrub through history. Hold SHIFT for a bigger step. The YEAR plate reads off the camera's real position, so it never lies either way.
  • YOU CANNOT GET LOST — fly past the edge of the data and it pulls you gently back; there is no blank paper to strand yourself in.
  • V or RESET VIEW — return to the river: face forward and slide back to the centre line, at whatever year you had reached. It lights up whenever you have strayed.
  • SEARCH (top right, or press /) — matches novel title, author, device name and description. Accents don't matter: Stanislaw finds Stanisław. Non-matches wash out to paper and stop responding; the counter tells you how many survived. It combines with UNBUILT ONLY rather than replacing it — both on means unbuilt and matching. ENTER on a narrow result flies you to the first hit.
  • ESC — closes the help, then the dossier, then clears the search, in that order. One press, one thing.
  • R — random jump to some corner of the four centuries.
  • HOVER — read a device name, and see the book it came out of where we have the cover.
  • CLICK — open the dossier. Realized ideas fly you down their thread to the year they became real.
  • DEEP INTO THE SCREEN — time. Everything you fly through is the four centuries; the YEAR plate reads your own position in them.
  • SIDEWAYS, UP AND DOWN — subject. The face of the river is a constellation of fourteen islands, one per domain, with open sky between them. It sweeps from raw matter (materials, energy, transport, space, on the left and over the top) through the made things that acquire a will of their own (weapons, robots, in the middle) and the body (food, medicine, genetics, low on the right) to pure information (mind, society, surveillance, communication, computing, up the right-hand side). An island is as big as it is crowded, and each runs the whole length of time — so a domain is a bundle of streams you can fly down. Ideas about the same thing knot together inside their island.
  • THE FAINT ROPES — bridges between neighbouring islands that keep turning up in the same novel. The nine strongest are drawn.
  • COLOUR — warm rust and orange means it came true. Cool teal and ink means still fiction — and those ones will not keep still: they drift and breathe where the realized ideas have settled. Height no longer means built or unbuilt; the colour and the threads say it instead, so the islands can have the whole cross-section to themselves.
  • THE FLOATING SIGN — names the island you are facing, hanging just above it. Turn your head and the next one lights up; the ones it is roped to ghost in beside it.
